The Stay or Leave tarot spread is a career tarot spread for the job you keep thinking about quitting. Five cards: one for what's actually bothering you, two for staying, and two for leaving. It lays out what each choice would ask of you, which helps at a career crossroads when you can't tell a bad week from a bad job.

## When to Use This Spread

Use it when "should I quit my job?" has been in your head for a while and you can't tell whether the job is the problem.

That might be Sunday dread that has lasted for months, or a new manager who changed the job you signed up for. It also works for a bigger career transition, when a new field or your own business is pulling at you and you haven't committed yet.

If you already have two clear options, like this offer or that one, the [Quick Decision spread](/free-tarot-spreads/quick-decision-tarot-spread) is a better fit. If the question is what kind of work you want at all, start with the [Career Path spread](/free-tarot-spreads/finding-your-career-path-job-tarot-card-spread).

"Should I quit?" is a yes or no question, and [tarot is bad at yes or no questions](/yes-or-no-tarot). This spread breaks it into a few questions better suited to tarot.

## The Card Positions

Put Card 1 in the center. Cards 2 and 3 go in a column on the left: that's the staying side. Cards 4 and 5 go in a column on the right: that's the leaving side.

**Card 1: The Real Problem**

*What is actually wearing on me?*

This card names the thing behind the urge to quit. It could be the work itself, the people, the pay, or something from outside work that's spilling into it.

The suit is a quick guide. Pentacles point to pay, security, or workload. Cups point to feeling unappreciated or cut off from the people around you. Swords point to conflict, stress, or office politics. Wands point to boredom or having no room to grow. A court card usually stands for a specific person, and it's often a boss.

Keep this card in mind for the rest of the reading. Both sides of the spread get checked against it.

**Card 2: Staying as It Is**

*What happens if nothing changes?*

This is the default path: you stay and keep doing what you're doing now.

People expect a disaster here, and this card is often milder than that. The Four of Cups in this spot shows a slow loss of interest. A dull job is easier to live with for a while than a miserable one, and that affects how fast you need to move.

**Card 3: Staying with a Change**

*What could I change without leaving?*

This card looks at options short of quitting: a talk with your manager, a different project, a move to another team, or firmer limits on your hours.

This option is easy to skip when you're fed up, so the spread builds it in. If this card is a lot stronger than Card 2, the problem may be fixable from where you are. The Page of Swords here points to a conversation you haven't had yet.

**Card 4: The Cost of Leaving**

*What would leaving ask of me?*

Every exit costs something: money, security, part of how you see yourself, work friendships, or the effort of a job search.

Read this card as a price to plan for. The Five of Pentacles here points to a lean stretch, so build savings before you give notice.

**Card 5: What You'd Be Leaving For**

*What am I moving toward?*

This card shows what's drawing you toward something new, if anything is.

If it's hard to read, you may know what you want to get away from without knowing where you want to go. That's worth finding out before you hand in your notice. The Three of Wands here is a clearer signal: someone looking out at what's coming, with a plan already in motion.

## Reading the Spread

Lay out all five cards before you read any of them.

Compare the two columns first. Major Arcana cards point to bigger forces, so if they gather on the leaving side, you may be looking at a full career transition and more than a job swap. A leaving side full of Pentacles means the question is mostly about money and security.

Then read Card 1 against each side. Does Card 3 answer the real problem? Does Card 5? If neither does, the problem may follow you to the next job. Burnout often works this way.

Say Card 1 is the Ten of Wands, someone carrying far too much. Card 3 is the Queen of Pentacles, and Card 5 is the Knight of Wands. Staying with a change points to a practical fix, like a manager who would back a lighter load. Leaving points to fast movement and new energy. Both sides answer the Ten of Wands, so the choice comes down to which one you have the appetite for.

## Tips for Beginners

If Card 1 is a court card, name the person before you read the rest. The spread reads differently once you know the problem is a person.

If Card 5 won't come clear, draw one more card, place it under Card 5, and ask "What would I want my next job to give me?"

If Card 3 suggests a conversation, have it, then do the reading again. Cards 2 and 3 often change once you know how your manager responds.

Write down the date and all five cards. A stay or leave decision can take months, and your first reading is useful to compare against later ones.

## A Note on Big Decisions

Quitting a job comes with money and benefits attached. This spread helps you sort out what you think and what you want. The practical checks, like savings and a next offer, happen outside the cards.
