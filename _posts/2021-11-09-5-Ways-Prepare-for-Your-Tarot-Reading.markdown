Most of what you get out of a tarot reading depends on the question you bring to it. A little prep before you sit down with a reader, in person or on a call, gives them more to work with and gives you a clearer answer.

If you're learning to read your own cards instead, start with [how to read tarot for beginners](/9-tarot-tips-for-beginners).

## 1. Write your questions down

Decide what you want to ask before the reading starts. A reader can do more with "What should I focus on at work this year?" than with "Will I get promoted?" There are [four guidelines for asking better tarot questions](/good-questions-for-tarot-readings/) if you want help opening a question up.

Put your questions in order of importance in case you run out of time. If you don't have a specific question, ask for a general reading. That's a normal request.

## 2. Pick the kind of reading

Readings happen in person, over a video or phone call, or in writing by email. A live reading lets you ask follow-up questions as the cards come up. A written reading gives you a record to reread and time to think before you reply.

For a call, find a quiet room and turn off notifications before it starts, so your attention stays on the reading.

## 3. Ask the reader how they work

Before you book, ask:

- How long the reading is, and how many questions it covers
- Whether they read for predictions or for reflection and advice
- Which topics they don't read on, since some readers turn down health, legal or third-party questions
- Which deck they use, and whether you can bring your own

## 4. Decide how much to tell them

Some people give the reader background on the situation first. Others give only the question and see what the cards bring up. Both work. Tell the reader which you'd like, so they know whether to ask you questions or read without context.

## 5. Say so when a card doesn't fit

A reading can point at a part of the situation you weren't asking about. If a card doesn't seem to fit, tell the reader and ask what they see in it. Hearing their reasoning often makes the card click, and it gives them a chance to adjust.

## 6. Record the reading or take notes

You'll forget more of a reading than you expect. Ask the reader before the session starts if you can record it. Some readers don't allow recording, so bring something to take notes on. A written reading comes with its own record.

Reread your notes a few weeks later. A reading can make more sense once the situation has moved on.

## Bonus tip: learn to read your own cards

A reading from someone else shows you how an experienced reader works through the cards. Reading your own cards takes that further. You already know the whole situation behind your question, so you can read each card against details no reader would know to ask about.

Over months, each card picks up your own history with it: the week it showed up before a job change, or the reading that only made sense later. That history is what makes reading for yourself so rewarding, and it grows every time you write a reading down. [How to read tarot for beginners](/9-tarot-tips-for-beginners) covers where to start.
