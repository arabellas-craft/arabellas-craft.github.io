In Ireland, the nights around Halloween were when the family dead came home. People swept the hearth, built up the fire, set chairs around it and left food and water on the table. The door was left on the latch so the souls could come in ([more on the custom here](/samhain-folk-customs)). Most houses did this on All Souls' Night, November 2nd, rather than on Halloween itself.

This four-card spread fits that stretch from Hallow Eve to All Souls'. One card is for what's at work around you, two are for your family's dead, and one is for the winter ahead.

## When to Use This Spread

Any night from October 31st to November 2nd. It's short, so it works when you want a reading but don't have an hour.

It's for your family line as a whole: the people your family came from, including the ones you never met. If you want to sit with one particular person you've lost, [Parting the Veil](/free-tarot-spreads/parting-the-veil-tarot-spread) gives that more room. If you'd rather play the old Hallow Eve fortune games, they're [collected here](/samhain-divination-games).

## The Card Positions

**Card 1: Beyond the Veil.**

What's at work around you that you can't see directly.

You can read this card as spirit, or as something moving under the surface of your life that you haven't named yet. The reading works either way. The Moon says things are unclear right now and you shouldn't trust first impressions. The High Priestess is something you already know but haven't said out loud. The Seven of Swords points to something someone is keeping from you.

**Cards 2 and 3: Ancestral Guidance.**

What your family's dead would want you to know. Card 2 is a message for now, and Card 3 is for the path ahead.

Ancestors here means more than names on a family tree. It includes what got handed down: how your family dealt with money, how it handled a fight, what it never talked about. A lot of the guidance in this position is about keeping some of that and dropping the rest.

The Ten of Pentacles is the family itself and what it built. The Queen of Pentacles is the practical one who kept the house running. The Five of Cups can point to a family habit of dwelling on loss, and a message to stop carrying it.

You don't need a specific person in mind. Some people find it easier to picture one grandparent, and others read for a whole side of the family. If one person keeps coming to mind through the reading, follow that up with Parting the Veil.

**Card 4: Winter Wisdom.**

What the coming winter brings: a lesson, a challenge, or an opportunity.

This is the practical card. The Four of Swords is rest you'll need. The Nine of Wands says you'll have to hold out for a while. The Ace of Pentacles is an opening, often a practical one like work or money.

## Reading the Spread

Lay Card 1 on the left, Card 2 at the top, Card 3 at the bottom, and Card 4 on the right.

Read Card 1 first, on its own. It tells you what's in the room.

Read Cards 2 and 3 together, top to bottom: the message for now, then the message for later. If they seem to disagree, look at where they pull apart before you look for a single message.

Read Card 4 last, against Cards 2 and 3. Advice from your family reads differently once you know what kind of winter you're heading into.

## Where the Veil Idea Comes From

The idea that the barrier between worlds thins at Samhain turns up in print in 1940. In the Irish folk record, the dead come home on All Souls' Night as the Holy Souls, let out of Purgatory for the night. [Five claims about Samhain](/halloween-origins-samhain-claims) traces both.

## Tips

Before you shuffle, leave a glass of water on the table and pull a chair up to the fire or the heater, the way Irish houses did. It's a small thing, and it sets the reading apart from an ordinary one.

Read for one side of the family at a time. Four cards can hold your mother's people or your father's people, but they get muddy trying to hold both.

Write the reading down and date it. Family readings tend to make more sense a few years later.

Any deck works. A worn deck, a cheap deck, a deck of ordinary playing cards ([how to read those](https://ko-fi.com/s/95f9c29692){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}).
